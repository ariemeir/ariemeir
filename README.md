<h1>Hi, I'm Arie 👋</h1>

I build systems that cross from software into the physical world, and I publish the
ones worth reading. Firmware, boards, enclosures, and the apps that drive them.

Most of them start the same way: something in the house or the shop is genuinely
annoying, I build the smallest version that fixes it, then keep going until it works
every day without my thinking about it.

Every project here carries a written case study, because the part worth reading is
usually the decision, not the code.

**Projects**

- **[WifeSignal](https://github.com/ariemeir/wifesignal)** · a traffic light for the
  office door, so one person can tell another how much this can wait. SwiftUI app, a
  BLE bridge on a Mac, ESP32-C3 firmware, a custom KiCad board, and an enclosure
  generated by a Fusion 360 script. In daily use.

- **[3D Scanner](https://github.com/ariemeir/3dscanner)** · a turntable photogrammetry
  rig that turns a physical object into a printable STL. An iPhone runs a camera server
  with a hand-rolled HTTP stack; an Arduino drives the turntable by impersonating its
  infrared remote.

- **[RoboImplant](https://github.com/ariemeir/roboimplant)** · a non-invasive adjustment
  system for a spinal distraction implant, from a research prototype at UC Berkeley and
  UCSF. AVR firmware, an Android clinician console over Bluetooth, a custom controller
  board, a handheld driver unit.

**How I work**

- Contracts first. The interesting failures live between subsystems, not inside them.
- Finished beats started. One thing at a time, until it survives daily use.
- Everything published carries its own case study and its own rights notice.

New work lands here as it finishes. If you are building something at the boundary
between hardware and software, or you want to pull one of these apart, say hello.
